## 1. Company

### What does the company do?

Precifarm AI Ltd is an EPRA-licensed Engineering, Procurement, Construction (EPC) and Operations and Maintenance (O&M) contractor delivering distributed solar PV and lithium energy storage systems across Kenya. We size every system against a written load assessment, install with our own crews, and carry a five-year service commitment on every deployment.

The product line is four standardised packages on a single modular hardware platform (Neura Pod), from a KSh 95,000 single-room Starter kit to multi-hundred-kWp Commercial Plus deployments for institutional anchor sites.

### Country of operation

Kenya. Headquartered in Nairobi with engineering hubs in five additional cities: Mombasa, Kisumu, Eldoret, Nakuru, and Nyeri. Operating coverage: nationwide.

### Company URL

[precifarm.com](https://precifarm.com)

### Incorporation date

August 2025. Incorporated as Precifarm AI Ltd under the Companies Act of Kenya.

- **EPRA Solar PV Vendor and Contractor Licence** (Energy and Petroleum Regulatory Authority) — covers Class T1, T2, and T3 system scope.
- **EBK Registered Graduate Engineer** (Engineers Board of Kenya) — Lead Engineer Sayia David Givondo, registration B25264.
- [VERIFY: NCA registration if applicable, KEBS certifications for hardware imports, county business permits]

### Operating thesis (three principles)

1. **EPC and O&M live in one team.** Most Kenyan installers sell EPC and walk away. We do not subcontract servicing. The crew that commissions a system is responsible for it through year five.
2. **Engineering over salesmanship.** Every system is sized against a written load assessment and a fixed scope. We do not close deals by promising what the system cannot deliver.
3. **Grow on revenue, not capital.** No outside investors to date. The company is structured to be cash-flow durable from year one. Capital from this round / grant accelerates a model that already pays its own bills.

## 3. Product

### What it is

A four-tier product line built on a single Neura Pod modular hardware platform:

| Package           | Capacity (PV + storage)         | Best for                                                   | Pricing                                          |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| **Starter**       | 550 W PV · 1.5 kWh storage      | Single-room homes, kiosks, renters, boda riders            | KSh 95,000 cash, or KSh 5,472/month (Lipa Pole Pole, 24 months) |
| **Family**        | 2.2 kWp PV · 5 kWh storage      | Two-to-three bedroom off-grid homes                        | KSh 290,000 cash. Bank financing available.      |
| **Commercial**    | Sized to site                   | Schools, clinics, SMEs, cooperatives                       | On quotation basis (written design + quote)      |
| **Commercial Plus** | Sized to site, multi-site      | Institutional anchor deployments (5 archetypes)            | On quotation basis (written design + quote)      |

Plus two extensions:

- **Solar Irrigation** sub-product: submersible borehole pumps, surface centrifugal pumps, and solar pump controllers, sized 1-acre smallholder to 100+ acre commercial farm.
- **EPRA T1/T2/T3 Training**: short-cohort programme preparing technicians and engineers for the EPRA solar PV licence application.

### How it works

1. **Site audit.** An engineer is dispatched from the regional hub responsible for the site. Roof, structural load, daily load profile, and grid interaction are measured, not estimated.
2. **Written design and quote.** Within two business days of the audit, the customer receives a sized recommendation, bill of quantities, and a firm price.
3. **Installation.** EPRA-licensed Precifarm crews, no subcontractors. Residential installs typically complete same-day. Commercial sequenced around customer operations.
4. **Five-year EPC + O&M commitment.** The same regional crew that commissioned the system services it through year five. Scheduled preventive maintenance, fault response, warranty management.

### What makes it different

- **Standardised packages on a single hardware platform.** Most Kenyan installers quote bespoke per-site. Standardisation lets us hold inventory, train technicians once, and amortise R&D across the fleet.
- **EPC + O&M as one obligation.** The five-year service commitment is contractually attached to the install, not sold as a separate add-on. This changes the design incentives: we will not install hardware we cannot service for five years.
- **Regional hub coverage, not Nairobi-only.** Six operating hubs mean response-time SLAs that a Nairobi-only competitor cannot match in Mombasa, Eldoret, Kisumu, Nakuru, or Nyeri.
- **Modular Neura Pod.** Customers upgrade through hardware swaps, not through rip-and-replace. The Starter customer who scales their load can become a Family customer without losing the original investment.
- **EV-ready from the inverter up.** Every package supports EV charging as an additive load without re-engineering the system.

## 4. Market

### Why solar in Kenya, why now

- **Solar irradiance**: 5 to 6 kWh/m²/day average across most of the country, among the highest globally. The resource is not the bottleneck.
- **Grid coverage**: KPLC connection has expanded under the Last Mile Connectivity Programme, but reliability remains the live problem. Documented frequent voltage dips and outages in commercial and rural areas drive both backup-storage demand and full off-grid demand.
- **Off-grid population**: [VERIFY: cite a recent published figure, e.g. World Bank, ESMAP, or KOSAP estimate of the share of Kenyan households without reliable grid access].
- **Lithium price trajectory**: Cell-level LFP pricing has fallen materially over the last five years, taking residential storage from an aspirational add-on to a unit-economic default. The Starter package at KSh 95,000 is below the price of a midrange petrol generator over its operating life.
- **Policy tailwinds**: Kenya's Vision 2030 targets universal access. The Energy Act 2019 established EPRA and the licensing framework. Government clean-energy commitments under the Nationally Determined Contributions (NDC) require scaled distributed solar to meet targets.
- **EV inflection**: Electric two- and three-wheeler adoption is accelerating, particularly in last-mile mobility. Charging infrastructure is the gating constraint; distributed solar with storage solves it without grid investment.

## 6. Business model and unit economics

### Revenue lines

1. **Hardware sale + installation (EPC margin).** Standard residential and SME tiers. Revenue recognised at commissioning.
2. **Five-year O&M contract (services margin).** Bundled into the install for residential. Annualised separately for Commercial Plus. Recurring.
3. **Lipa Pole Pole financing margin.** Starter tier: KSh 12,500 deposit, KSh 5,472/month over 24 months, ~KSh 143,800 total. Margin on the financing spread over the cash price (KSh 95,000) accounts for credit risk and time value.

## 7. Competition

### Direct competitors

- **Large-format Kenyan EPC contractors** (e.g. [VERIFY: specific named competitors David wants to call out]). Strong on the Nairobi commercial market; thinner regional coverage; typically subcontract O&M; bespoke per-site rather than standardised packages.
- **Off-grid solar product distributors** (M-KOPA, Sun King, Greenlight Planet, d.light, BBOXX). Strong on the entry-tier residential market with pay-as-you-go financing. Hardware-led, not engineering-led; do not service Commercial or Commercial Plus loads; SHS-scale only.
- **International EPCs entering the Kenyan market**. Generally focused on utility-scale grid-connected PV or DFI-financed mini-grids; not in the distributed C&I and residential segments Precifarm targets.

### Why Precifarm wins where it competes

- **Five-year EPC + O&M on every package.** The competition wins on price-per-watt at install; we win on price-per-served-hour over five years.
- **Six-city footprint with resident engineers.** Competitors quote Nairobi-only response times. We have an engineer in Mombasa, Kisumu, Eldoret, Nakuru, Nyeri.
- **Standardised packages on a single hardware platform.** Inventory, training, and after-sales support all amortise across the fleet. Bespoke shops cannot match the per-install cost structure at volume.
- **EV-ready architecture from day one.** Most installed-base systems in Kenya cannot accept EV charging loads without re-engineering. Ours can.

### Moat trajectory

1. **Operational moat (today).** The six-hub footprint and the EPC + O&M discipline are operationally hard to replicate without years of crew-building.
2. **Service-incumbency moat (year 2-3).** Every Precifarm install on the five-year commitment is a customer the competition cannot poach mid-cycle. Compounds with deployment count.
3. **Platform moat (year 3+).** Standardisation + OTA-updatable firmware + cumulative field telemetry will produce a service-cost advantage that bespoke installers structurally cannot match.

## 8. Why now

- **Lithium cell price has crossed the threshold** at which residential storage is a default rather than a luxury. Starter and Family unit economics work today that did not work three years ago.
- **EPRA licensing framework matured** under the 2019 Energy Act. The market structure now rewards licensed and accredited operators; informal installers face increasing customer scrutiny and regulatory enforcement.
- **Kenya grid reliability remains a documented constraint** that distributed solar with storage directly solves, both for backup and for full off-grid.
- **EV adoption is at the inflection** where charging infrastructure becomes the gating bottleneck, and Precifarm's EV-ready architecture is positioned to serve it before competitors retrofit.
- **Bank-financing channels are open**: Equity, KCB Clean Energy, Stanbic Solar PV, and Co-op Bank actively underwrite distributed solar against documented engineering quotes. The capital stack exists; the engineering capacity to deploy against it does not, at scale.
- **No incumbent dominates the C&I and residential-with-storage segment**. The market is fragmented; consolidation is overdue; the operator who builds the operational platform first compounds.
